Weekly Highlights

My favourite results I discovered each week!

15 Sept - Sept 21, 2025

I was trying to solve the following exercise

Exercise (Hartshorne's I.1.9) Let $I \triangleleft k[x_1, \ldots, x_n]$ be generated by $r$ elements, then every irreducible component of $Z(I)$ has dimension at most $n-r$

Which lead me to conjecture the following result, which is a generalisation of Hartshorne's theorem $I.1.11A.$

Theorem (Krull's Hauptidealsatz) Let $A$ be a Noetherian commutative ring and $J \triangleleft A$ be generated by $r$ elements. Then every minimal prime ideal containing $J$ has height at most $r$.

I'm specially happy I managed to prove the theorem myself and that I conjectured it alone.

Now let's move to projective closures. If $Y$ is an affine variety specifying $I(\overline{Y})$ is not actually that straightforward. The immediate first guess is just the homogeneification of $I(Y)$ but that turns out to be too much, indeed the homogeneification need not be prime because it introduces "junk at infinity".

Homogenize then saturate: Say $Y \subset \mathbb{A}^n_{(x_1, \ldots, x_n)}$ is an affine variety and $\overline{Y} \subset \mathbb{P}^n_{(t:x_1, \ldots, x_n)}$ is its projective closure. Define the homogenizor with respect to $t$ to be the map $H_t:k[x_1, \ldots, x_n] \to k[t, x_1, \ldots, x_n]^h$ such that $H_t(f) = t^{deg(f)}f(x_1/t, \ldots, x_n/t)$. Then $$I(\overline{Y}) = (\langle H_t(I(U)) \rangle : t^{\infty})$$ The saturation with respect to $t$ of the homogeneification of all members of $I(Y)$.

I don't know how usual is the definition of saturation, but what I mean by the above is $\{f \in k[t, x_1, \ldots, x_n| \exists m \in \mathbb{N}_0; t^mf \in \langle H_t(I(U)) \rangle\}$

22 sept - 28 sept 2025

Let $Y = Z(x^3 - xy - y^3) \subset \mathbb{A}^n_{(x,y)}$, I was trying to understand the the strict transform of $Y$. In particular if we looked at the strict transform $\overline{Y} \subset \mathbb{A}^n_{(x,y)} \times \mathbb{P}^{n-1}_{(s:t)}$ what is its intersection with the excepcional subset $E$ ? I'm constantly frustrated by how complicated it is to solve "computational" exercises like this one. I believe one can find $I(\overline{Y})$ explicitly by working with saturations but I don't know how to do that, so instead I just tried a bunch of equations until I got to something promising. If we define $Y' = Z(xt - ys, xs^2 + st + yt^2)$ then $Y' \setminus E = \overline{Y} \setminus E$ hence $Y' \supset \overline{Y}$ and $Y' \cap E$ is just two points. Now the miraculous argument, since $Y$ is defined by equations symetric on $x$ and $y$ so must be $\overline{Y}$, since we know $((0,0),(1:1)) \notin Y' \supset \overline{Y}$ this means $E \cap \overline{Y}$ must have even cardinality if its finite. But we know it is finite since it can be at maximum 2 (the cardinality of $Y' \cap E$) and can't be $0$ so it is $2$ and $\overline{Y} = Y'$.

The emotions I felt when I saw this are indescribable, truly worthy of the title gem.
