Sisyphus and Boredom

I believe motivation to pursue goals is either goal driven which is senseless, or is boredom avoidance disguised very nicely. Think of Sisyphus (from The Myth of Sisyphus ), he is condemned to a meaningless task for eternity as a punishment. This makes us ponder on the meaning of tasks we do in our day to day lives. As one goes deeper into the why he eventually gets to a dead end. Somewhere one needs to say “I do this for others” or “I don’t know why” (this “I don’t know why” is characteristic of non-axiomatic theories, but I digress). No one is profoundly motivated by the former, it is just a “say what is correct” way to escape the question. So if we accept we don’t know why we do things in what precise way are we different to Sisyphus?

That difference is that we are not obligated to push any rocks up hills, no god condemned us to do so. We can come to accept that no rock needs to be pushed, that it is all meaningless and need not be done. But one should not accept this from a depressive lens “if nothing needs to be done there is no reason to live”, but rather from the way of nature, from total acceptance. Not only acceptance, but love of one’s cosmic irrelevance, love for the truth that each individual is merely a part of a gigantic eternal system called nature. Love that that if he does nothing or does everything it barely matters. This is what enlightment is to me actually, but I digress.

Ok, say we’ve accepted that no rock needs to be pushed (enlightment or not). It seems then completely justified to live a life of leisure. But there is one problem with leisure, it is boring. So one needs to choose between doing something meaningless and being bored, I would pick anything over boredom so pushing the rock it is. But now we are not pushing the rock to get it to the top, we are pushing it only as a way to avoid the ultimate suffering, which is boredom. However recall that once the rock is pushed up the hill we will throw it down again so for one to push the rock thinking about getting it to the top is no different to a dog trying to hunt down its own tail. So pushing the rock is meaningless but not objectiveless, it has the sole objective of avoiding boredom.

This reasoning, although convincing to me, is pretty grim. According to our discussion It is correct to say all one does in life is to avoid boredom, but this isn’t very motivating. One might argue that reality isn’t motivating, it is just reality and motivation is a futile construction of the human mind. But here I thing it is useful to slightly turve our glasses of reality and accept the futile inherent in our human nature. We will abuse the impressive power humans have of creating narratives and convincing themselves of them. Lets tell ourselves the story that we love pushing the rock up the hill and that is why we push it, not for getting it to the top (which makes no sense), or to avoid boredom (which is true but not useful). This sounds crazy in the case of Sisyphus, but the activities we are grouping together in the rock up the hill metaphor are almost always inherently fun (at least slightly) so our story will be fairly convincing.

One might make the point that the wise man searches for truth and so turving our glasses of reality should be avoided at all costs. But to believe one will get motivated from boredom avoidance is arguably an even bigger turving. At the end of the day I’m willing to tell myself a story I once knew was distorted if it leads to a happier life and I think so would be the wise man.
